# 📈 Hydrogen Demand in Germany

> Hydrogen isn't a fuel of the future — it's a massive industrial market today. Almost 100 million tonnes are consumed globally every year, and Germany alone needs 55 TWh just to keep its existing industry running. The question isn't whether hydrogen will be used, but who will produce it and at what carbon intensity.

---

## Global Demand: The Numbers

Global hydrogen demand reached **~100 million tonnes (Mt)** by 2024 — a 2% increase from 2023 (IEA, 2025). To put that in perspective: ammonia production, one of hydrogen's biggest consumers, uses ~150 million tonnes of feedstock annually.

The demand structure reveals where hydrogen already matters:

| Sector | 2024 demand | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Refineries (oil & gas) | >43 Mt | ~43% |
| Industry (ammonia, methanol, DRI) | ~55 Mt | ~55% |
| New applications (biofuels, etc.) | <1 Mt | <1% |

**Where does industrial demand go?**
- ~60% → ammonia production
- ~30% → methanol production
- ~10% → Direct Reduced Iron (DRI) in steelmaking

**Who consumes the most?** China is the largest single consumer with over 29 Mt — more than a quarter of global demand — though it is rapidly building domestic production capacity.

> [!note] RA
> The striking part: "new applications" (fuel cells, power, heating) are still under 1% of demand. The hydrogen economy of the future is real — but today's market is entirely industrial.

---

## Why Refineries Can't Run Without Hydrogen

Refineries are hydrogen-hungry because modern fuel standards demand it. Hydrogen is used in:

- **Hydrotreating** — removing sulfur and impurities
- **Hydrocracking** — upgrading heavy viscous feedstocks into lighter products
- **Hydroprocessing** — improving fuel quality

The demand intensity depends on the crude quality:

| Feedstock type | Hydrogen demand |
|---|---|
| Viscous / heavy crude | 400–1,200 scf per barrel |
| Non-viscous / light crude | <200 scf per barrel |

Many refineries now operate **on-site hydrogen production plants** just to supply their own processes — they can't buy enough from the merchant market (Speight, 2024).

---

## Germany: The Numbers That Matter

Germany's current hydrogen demand is **55 TWh per year**. The official forecast for 2030: **95–130 TWh** — roughly a doubling to 2.4x current consumption (IEA, 2025).

The **National Hydrogen Strategy** is the policy backbone:

- Production, transport, and use framework with generous incentives
- Primarily targeted at **green hydrogen**, with a recent shift to also support **blue hydrogen**
- Electrolyzer capacity target **doubled to 10 GW by 2030** (BMWK, 2023)

Germany is a "high-intensity hydrogen resource country" — its chemical industry, steel mills, and refineries are among Europe's largest hydrogen consumers. The planned **Hydrogen Network 2030** (FNB Gas) maps the pipeline routes needed to distribute this resource nationwide.

---

## Why Demand Drives Project Feasibility

For an industrial hydrogen project (like an SMR+CCS plant), demand is the foundation of the business case:

1. **Local demand determines plant scale** — how big to build, how fast to ramp up
2. **Off-take contracts decide bankability** — investors need credible counterparties willing to sign long-term agreements
3. **Industrial clusters shape infrastructure** — pipeline, storage, and CO₂ transport options depend on where demand clusters
4. **Utilization rate sets economics** — a plant running at 60% vs 90% capacity has very different unit costs

The key insight: hydrogen demand in Germany is **not the bottleneck**. The question is whether credible stakeholders will sign long-term contracts at a price that covers production costs — and that depends on carbon pricing, policy support, and the competitive landscape.
