# ⚡ Redispatch 2.0 — How Germany Manages Grid Bottlenecks

> When electricity can't flow from where it's produced to where it's consumed, grid operators must intervene. **Redispatch** is that intervention — and since October 2021, Redispatch 2.0 has completely changed which plants participate.

---

## The Problem: Bottlenecks

Germany's energy transition moved generation away from consumption centers — wind farms in the north, industry in the south. The grid wasn't built for that direction of flow. When transmission lines hit their limits, the system needs **bottleneck management**: reducing output on one side, increasing it on the other, so the grid stays stable.

---

## What Changed with Redispatch 2.0

The **Network Expansion Acceleration Act (NABEG)** added new requirements for bottleneck prevention and management. Since **October 1, 2021**, the EnWG (§§13a and 14) defines Redispatch 2.0:

| Aspect | Before | Redispatch 2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| **Participating plants** | Mostly conventional generators | **All generators ≥ 100 kW** — including renewables, storage, CHP |
| **Renewables** | Excluded | Included |
| **Storage & CHP** | Excluded | Included |
| **Goal** | Fix immediate bottlenecks | Efficient, non-discriminatory bottleneck elimination |

The plant pool grew dramatically — every wind turbine and solar park above 100 kW is now part of the redispatch toolkit.

---

## How It Works

- **Conventional plants first:** renewable systems are only dispatched when conventional options are exhausted
- **Cost threshold:** renewables step in when their bottleneck-removal costs are **10× lower** than conventional, or **5× lower** than CHP
- **Compensation:** participating plants are compensated — but grid operators only pay a **standard market premium**

This ordering keeps costs down while maximizing the use of available capacity.

---

## Why It Matters

1. **Grid integration of renewables** — more plants in the pool means more flexibility to manage the grid
2. **Higher efficiency** — a broader, area-covering plant pool beats the old case-by-case approach
3. **Cost control** — targets the steadily rising costs of redispatch and network connection management
4. **Fairness** — non-discriminatory treatment of all generation types

---

## Connection to the Electricity Market

Redispatch is the physical reality behind the market design described in [Merit-Order-System](/Wirtschaft/Merit-Order-System): the market sets prices, but when the physics says "no," redispatch reconciles the two. It's a core reason why German electricity prices include grid-cost components beyond the pure energy price.

**Look at your next electricity bill** — the grid-cost line is redispatch at work. Want to understand German energy prices? Start there.
